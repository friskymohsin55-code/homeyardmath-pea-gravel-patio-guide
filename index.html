How Much Pea Gravel Does a 20×10 Patio Really Need?

Ordering pea gravel by guess is how you end up with a driveway full of leftover bags — or a patio that runs short halfway through. The math is simple, and the pea gravel calculator does it for any size, but here is the worked example so you can check it yourself.

The worked example: 20 ft × 10 ft patio, 3 inches deep

Volume first: 20 × 10 × (3 ÷ 12) = 50 cubic feet. Divide by 27 and you get 1.85 cubic yards. Pea gravel weighs about 100 lbs per cubic foot, or 2,700 lbs per cubic yard, so that is 5,000 lbs — 2.50 US tons.

Then add the 10% waste allowance for uneven ground and settling: 1.85 × 1.10 = 2.04 cubic yards, or 55 cubic feet and 5,500 lbs (2.75 tons). Order this number, not the neat one — a patio that is actually 22 ft long instead of 20 ft needs 10% more stone.

One cubic yard takes 54 standard 50-lb bags, so bagged gravel runs about $269 per cubic yard against $52 bulk. The breakeven point is just 0.39 cubic yards — about 21 bags. Anything bigger than a 42 sq ft bed at 3 inches deep is cheaper in bulk. Bags are only for small beds and top-ups.

Depth and install tips

Three inches is the standard for patios and walkways. Go thinner than 2 inches and the black fabric shows through; go past 3.5 inches and the round stones start to feel like quicksand underfoot. Keep the depth even, and install rigid edging at least 1.5 inches above the gravel line before you spread a single stone — otherwise the first mowing sends gravel across the lawn. Never pour pea gravel straight onto soft soil: lay a 3–4 inch compacted crushed stone base first, or the round stones sink into mud after the first wet season.

Remember: one cubic yard covers about 108 sq ft at 3 inches deep. Divide any bed's square footage by 108 to check your order before you pay.
